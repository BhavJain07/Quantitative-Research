# QuantNQ Research Papers

**Bhav Jain · [quantnq.com](https://quantnq.com)**

Four working papers on market structure, trading risk, and the conversion of trading profits into spendable cash. Each paper develops its economic question through mathematical definitions, quantitative analysis, and supporting visualizations.

## Papers

### 1. Contract Surplus and the Economics of External Hedging

**[Read the paper →](hedging.pdf)**

Examines the economics of externally hedging an account with loss limits, payout conditions, and a finite deadline. Separates the value of the underlying contractual right from the contribution of the hedge itself.

- Finite-expiry valuation and barrier-based payoff structures.
- Qualification-aware policies and cash-matched controls.
- Settlement delays, financing requirements, and liquidity risk.
- Analytical results alongside controlled synthetic experiments.

### 2. Order Flow under a One-Position Constraint

**[Read the paper →](orderflow.pdf)**

Studies a fifteen-signal Nasdaq futures portfolio in which signals compete for a single available position. Connects signal construction to allocation, ownership, and realized portfolio contribution.

- Volume profiles, time-at-price structures, VWAP, and order-flow signals.
- Information clocks, entry conditions, and overlap arbitration.
- Conditional contribution versus standalone strategy performance.
- Historical return paths, drawdowns, costs, and recent-session economics.

### 3. Options Concentrations as Exit Information

**[Read the paper →](optionsflow.pdf)**

Investigates whether options-derived concentrations are useful for managing an existing trade, separately from their ability to predict an entry or reversal.

- Gamma- and delta-conditioned profit targets.
- Paired comparisons against unchanged exits and target-geometry controls.
- Snapshot availability and execution timing.
- Native-NDX level contacts, price reactions, and final-extreme classification.

### 4. From Trade Profits to Spendable Cash

**[Read the paper →](payout.pdf)**

Evaluates high-trade-frequency, low-reward-to-risk strategies through the economics of funded-account payouts. The objective is modeled spendable cash—not simply win rate, trade expectancy, or account balance.

- Account survival, payout eligibility, and settlement.
- Acquisition and replacement costs.
- Endpoint accounting versus chronological account replay.
- Dependence across accounts, cash-flow concentration, and path sensitivity.

## About

These papers bring together research on three connected questions:

1. **What information does a trading rule use?**
2. **How do execution and portfolio constraints change its economics?**
3. **How much of the resulting profit can become usable cash?**

The repository contains the PDF editions. Further research and analysis are available at **[quantnq.com](https://quantnq.com)**.

## Research status

These are working papers, not peer-reviewed publications. Results are historical, analytical, or simulated as specified in each paper; modeled payouts are not verified cash receipts. Research only—not investment advice.
